Claude output re a possible new Marketing Spend agent:



Here's my full analysis and recommendations for the new agent, given it's a sale-specific WS2
agent (feeding both Sale Readiness Consulting and the M&A process).

Recommended name

"Marketing Spend & Performance Agent" (WS2)

Rationale: it mirrors the simple, functional naming of the existing "Digital Presence Agent," and
"spend & performance" (rather than just "spend") signals it covers not just dollars but efficiency,
tracking maturity, and transferability — which matter more to a buyer than raw budget numbers.

What it should cover

Since this is sale-specific, the frame isn't "how do we improve marketing" — it's "how does a
buyer assess marketing as a transferable, revenue-generating asset." That reframes the scope:

   ●​ Paid search (SEO + SEM) — organic visibility for local search terms, website ranking
      and SEO, Google Business Profile optimization (usually the single biggest lead driver for
      a hyper-local service business), plus actual Google Ads spend + additional fees if
      applicable (agency, etc), structure, keywords, and conversion tracking
   ●​ Paid social — Meta/TikTok ad spend specifically (budget levels, campaign types,
      boosted posts vs. real campaigns) — the piece the Digital Presence agent explicitly
      doesn't touch
   ●​ Other paid channels common to pet care — Rover/Wag/Yelp Ads/Nextdoor, directory
      listings
   ●​ Spend as % of revenue — benchmarked against comparable service businesses; both
      "too low" (under-investment) and "too high" (inefficiency/dependency) are diligence flags
   ●​ Channel mix / revenue attribution — how much revenue is paid-dependent vs.
      organic/referral/repeat — buyers discount businesses that need ever-increasing ad
      spend to sustain revenue. List other client sources like events, sponsorships, referrals,
      etc.
   ●​ Email list — Most of the time this is a big revenue driver. How many subscribers, how
      many active, open rate, CTR, strategy. Highlights red flags or opportunities.
   ●​ Vendor/team structure — in-house vs. agency vs. owner doing it all personally
      (key-person risk). When the team (not the owner) manages marketing in-house, that's a
      green flag!
   ●​ Documented plan/budget — written annual marketing plan vs. ad hoc spend, same
      with goals: are there any set, and do they measure against them?

Relationship to the Digital Presence Agent
Option A — Standalone, paired agent (spend/SEO/paid only; leave Digital Presence as-is)

   ●​ Pros: fast to build, zero risk to a working agent, clean separation of concerns
      (content/organic vs. financial/paid), fits your existing pattern of many discrete specialized
      agents
   ●​ Cons: two outputs to manually synthesize per engagement; risk of overlapping or slightly
      inconsistent findings (e.g., both agents commenting on Instagram)

Option B — Unified agent that absorbs Digital Presence and retires it

   ●​ Pros: one complete marketing picture (organic + paid + spend) — best for CIM/buyer
      narrative, no redundancy
   ●​ Cons: requires rebuilding and re-validating something that already works; bigger,
      harder-to-maintain spec; breaks the modular pattern across your ~29 agents

Option C — New agent orchestrates the Digital Presence agent as an input, then layers
spend/SEO on top

   ●​ Pros: best of both — a single synthesized output when you want it, but each piece stays
      independently maintainable; doesn't touch the working agent; scales cleanly if you add
      more marketing-adjacent agents later (email, local SEO, reviews)
   ●​ Cons: more upfront complexity (needs a composition/hand-off step, not just a prompt);
      depends on the Digital Presence agent's output being structured enough to feed
      downstream reliably

My recommendation: build it as Option A now, but design its output schema so it can be
merged with the Digital Presence agent's output later — i.e., aim at Option C as the
medium-term target rather than committing to a full orchestration layer before you know it's
worth the build cost. This gets you spend coverage fastest, doesn't put the working agent at risk,
and keeps the door open to a unified view once you've run it on a few real engagements. I'd
only jump straight to B if you're fairly confident the current Digital Presence agent needs a
rework anyway.

How it should gather data

1. Owner questionnaire only — fast, no access/security complexity, but self-reported numbers
are unverifiable and most independent owners don't actually track CAC or spend-by-channel, so
you'd get thin data.

2. Direct account access (Google Ads, Meta Ads Manager, GA4) — the most accurate and
buyer-credible numbers, but this runs into real friction: it requires the owner to grant OAuth-level
access, isn't something Cantara should be handling via stored credentials, and is a heavy build
for a boutique firm before you know this needs to be a repeatable, scaled product.

3. Hybrid — structured questionnaire + owner-provided exports/screenshots (Meta Ads
Manager summary, Google Ads dashboard, GA4/GBP insights) — real numbers without
touching credentials, plus the questionnaire captures the qualitative diligence flags (who
manages it, is there a plan, who owns the accounts) that raw numbers alone won't surface.
Main downside is it's still manual/asynchronous, but that's true of the financial intake you
already do.

4. Documents Cantara already collects — zero new owner effort, but financials only show one
aggregate "marketing expense" line — not enough to assess channel mix, SEO health, or ad
efficiency.

My recommendation: Option 3 (hybrid). This is a point-in-time sale-readiness assessment,
not an always-on tactical tool, so a structured intake is the right weight — it gets defensible
numbers without a credentials/security problem, and it's the option most likely to surface the
findings that actually matter for a sale (account ownership, documentation, key-person
dependency) rather than just a spend total. Treat direct account access (Option 2) as a possible
phase-2 enhancement once you've validated the questionnaire approach across a few
engagements.

What it should produce

1. Scored/rubric assessment — fast to scan, easy to benchmark across clients over time,
clean red/yellow/green diligence flags — but reductive on its own, and your scoring criteria won't
feel credible until you've built a comparables library.

2. Narrative report — reads well for the owner, but harder to scan and harder to reuse in
CIM/valuation work.

3. Structured data only, feeding the Roadmap/CIM — most reusable and composable across
your other deliverables, but not directly handoff-able to an owner without an extra formatting
step.

4. Combination — internal scored assessment + owner-facing narrative summary

My recommendation: Option 4, since this agent explicitly needs to serve two audiences —
internal use (feeding CIM/valuation and cross-engagement benchmarking as your agent library
matures) and the owner conversation itself, consistent with how your other named deliverables
(like the Sale Readiness Roadmap) work. If you want to ship something simpler first, build the
scored rubric alone as v1 — it's the more foundational, reusable piece — and add the narrative
layer once you've run it live and know what's actually worth explaining to owners.

Gaps you may be missing

A few things that fall outside "SEO / ad spend / social" as stated but matter a lot in a pet-resort
sale context:
   ●​ Reviews & reputation management — sits between your two agents (not "content
      posting," not "ad spend") but is often the single biggest driver of bookings for a local
      service business. Someone needs to own this.
   ●​ Referral and vet-partner channels — for many pet resorts this is the largest channel by
      revenue, and it's entirely outside both "digital presence" and "ad spend" framing. Worth a
      dedicated line item.
   ●​ Booking funnel/conversion health, not just traffic — does the site actually convert
      visits into bookings (online booking widget, phone tracking), since spend efficiency is
      meaningless without this.
   ●​ Seasonality — boarding demand spikes around holidays, so spend efficiency should be
      read against seasonal patterns, not a flat annual average.
   ●​ Key-person dependency — is the owner personally running all of this with no
      documented process, which ties directly into Cantara's core "can this run without the
      current owner" thesis.
