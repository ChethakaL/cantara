## SECTION 7 — BUYER-FACING OBLIGATIONS SUMMARY

**Workforce Overview:** Foothills Pet Resort employs 12 individuals plus the owner-operator, with 8 full-time and 4 part-time employees across kennel operations, grooming, and front desk functions. Only the Operations Manager has a formal employment agreement; all other employees operate under at-will handbook policies. The workforce is typical for a mid-sized pet resort operation.

**Non-Compete Protections:** The seller has executed a comprehensive 24-month non-compete and non-solicitation agreement covering a 25-mile radius, providing strong buyer protection. However, only the Operations Manager has any restrictive covenant (employee non-solicitation only), creating significant risk that key employees in specialized roles (Head Groomer, Lead Kennel Tech, Front Desk Supervisor) could immediately compete or be recruited by competitors post-close.

**Assumed Benefit Obligations:** Under an asset sale structure, the buyer is not required to assume existing benefit obligations but will need to establish new benefit programs to retain employees. The current employer-paid health insurance costs approximately $28,800 annually for full-time employees. All benefits are discretionary under handbook policies, giving the buyer flexibility in plan design. Arizona statutory sick leave obligations will transfer to the buyer as a matter of law.

**Retirement Plan & PTO Obligations:** Retirement plan status is unclear due to missing plan documents, creating uncertainty about potential assumption obligations or termination requirements. Accrued PTO liability cannot be quantified without balance reports but could represent significant hidden liability if employees have substantial unused balances. Arizona law may require PTO payout upon separation.

**Independent Contractor Risk:** No independent contractor relationships identified in current payroll, eliminating misclassification exposure from existing arrangements. However, if the business historically used 1099 workers, those relationships should be reviewed for potential retroactive liability.

**Transition Considerations:** Under asset sale structure, the buyer must make new employment offers to all existing employees. The lack of employment agreements for key personnel creates both opportunity (flexibility in new terms) and risk (no retention mechanisms). The owner-operator model means the buyer will need to either hire replacement management or assume direct operational oversight. Arizona does not have state-level WARN Act requirements, and the workforce size (12 employees) is below federal WARN Act thresholds.

**Items Requiring Buyer's Employment Counsel Review:**
• Health insurance plan transferability and COBRA obligations
• Retirement plan status and potential termination/assumption requirements  
• Accrued PTO liability quantification and payout obligations under Arizona law
• Employment agreement templates for key employee retention
• Non-compete/non-solicitation agreements for critical roles post-close
• Compliance with Arizona employment law requirements for new employer
